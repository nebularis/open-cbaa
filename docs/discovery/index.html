Vision & proof of value

The agreement is the rulebook.
Write it once.

Today a binding authority agreement is a document. Every party that acts on it — MGA oversight, coverholder platforms, bordereau validation, claims authority matrices — re-encodes its rules, and every encoding can drift from the wording and from each other.

Meaning is attached once to each library wording object, bound per agreement from its variables, and compiled into every operational form from one intermediate representation. Every check, referral and report comes from the agreement, and cites it. design-spec §3.4 · integration spec §6.2

Where things stand

A data standard, and the gap beside it

Blueprint Two and the Core Data Record are today's reference points†. They standardise the data exchanged — what fields exist, and their formats — not the rules that govern them. A demonstration has to show semantics doing work that a data standard cannot.

The CDR standardises the data exchanged, not the rules that govern it. Participants still interpret and re-implement the rules themselves.
→
The rules themselves are data, derived from the contract.
Validation checks structure — is the field present, well formed — not contract: is this risk within this binder.
→
Contractual validation, with the clause that failed.
The cost of capture falls on coverholders and brokers, while the benefit accrues to central processing — tax, regulatory reporting, settlement.
→
Value for the coverholder and the managing agent, on day one.
Built around open-market placement. Delegated authority stays on monthly bordereaux, so out-of-authority business is found weeks or months after binding.
→
Authority checked at the point of bind. Bordereaux become confirmation, not discovery.

Where to start

The minimum slice

Acts 2, 4 and 5, over M5 alone

Bind, Amend and Audit make a complete proof on their own. Act 1 needs inclusion conditions and a settled treatment of case-vs-scheme wildcards (I8). Act 3 needs accumulators. Act 6 needs the Behaviour lifecycles. None of those block the minimum slice.

Needed before it runs, for real
  • LATTICE imports: Foundation, Vocabulary, Party, Eligibility, Instrument
  • Territory, insurable interest and peril schemes reclassified as SKOS
  • AuthorityGrant statements attached to the SoUA wording
  • The compilation module (integration spec §9, steps 2–5)
  • I7 settled — the case is the risk, with a single-valued deemed location
  • I9 settled for the territory scheme — sibling disjointness where it's a tree
Known tensions
V1 — a demo is executable, but AP1 says no reference implementation before the design is complete

Either build the demo only from tools already permitted under D6 (compilers, validators, a notebook), or record a scoped AP1 exception for a demo kept outside ontology/.

V2 — which audience comes first: oversight teams, coverholders, the LMA's CBAA Steer, or the contract builders

The acts stay the same; the emphasis shifts. Oversight cares about Acts 3–5, coverholders about Act 2, the LMA about the whole thread.

V3 — use of LMA material

The CBAA drafts are shared for education. A public demo may need the LMA's agreement, or synthetic wording built to the same shape.

V4 — the Blueprint Two and CDR comparison needs verifying against current market sources

Stated here from the ideation note as written. It should not be repeated to a market audience before it's checked against what Blueprint Two and the CDR currently claim for themselves.

V5 — bordereau format

Lloyd's coverholder reporting standards define the rows. The demo needs a mapping from one real layout — where MORK's validated mappings apply.
